Patient Guide

Frequently Asked Questions

Clear answers to common questions about surgical procedures, consultations, and pregnancy care.

Gallstones that cause symptoms (such as pain in the upper right abdomen, bloating after meals, or nausea) require surgical removal of the gallbladder (laparoscopic cholecystectomy). Medicines cannot reliably dissolve gallstones or prevent future attacks, pancreatitis, or infection. Asymptomatic "silent" stones found incidentally usually only need observation, unless specific high-risk features are present.
Because laparoscopic surgery uses small 5mm to 10mm incisions rather than large open cuts, post-operative pain is minimal. Most patients walk comfortably within hours of surgery, are discharged within 24 to 48 hours, and can resume desk work and light routine activities within 5 to 7 days.
We provide comprehensive surgical care for malignancies of the entire digestive tract — including cancers of the stomach, gallbladder, liver, pancreas, colon, and rectum. Treatment emphasizes thorough radiological staging, laparoscopic minimally invasive approaches where indicated, and evidence-based radical resection.
Your first visit includes a comprehensive medical and family health review, baseline vital checks, calculation of gestational age and expected delivery date, prescription of essential prenatal vitamins (folic acid), and planning of routine first-trimester scans (Dating / NT scan) and blood tests.
No. Small, asymptomatic fibroids and simple ovarian cysts frequently require only routine ultrasound monitoring. Surgery (laparoscopic myomectomy or cystectomy) is considered only if there is severe pelvic pain, heavy menstrual bleeding leading to anemia, rapid growth, or interference with fertility.
